Growth is a sizing problem.

Size the stake,
not the story.

Kelly maximizes expected log growth in a known model. That is a different goal from maximizing average wealth, avoiding drawdowns or knowing your edge.

Chances 10-90%. Net payoff 0.5-3. Stake 0-95%. Seed 1-4,294,967,295. A loss forfeits the stake; a win returns the stake plus the net payoff. Everything is hypothetical.

01

Known odds, repeated stakes

A trial wins with the model chance and loses otherwise. Net payoff b means a winning unit earns b units of profit. Each strategy stakes a fixed share f of its current wealth. The rest stays idle. Assumed chance chooses the two Kelly stakes; model chance drives the curve and every simulated outcome.

02

Log growth is not average wealth

g(f) = p ln(1 + bf) + (1 - p) ln(1 - f). With no borrowing or short bets, f* = max(0, (pb - (1 - p))/b). The horizon does not enter that formula. A high arithmetic mean can be driven by rare large outcomes. Log-optimal does not mean low drawdown or best on every path.

03

Fractional is not a safety label

Half Kelly cuts the assumed full-Kelly stake in half. Neither one knows whether the assumed chance is right. An overestimated edge can turn expected log growth negative. Even a correctly specified Kelly stake can suffer large drawdowns.

Simulation and numerical choices

128 paths share the same seeded xorshift32 outcomes across all three stakes. One uniform draw per trial, win if draw < model chance. Pseudo-random outcomes approximate an independent binary model; the finite generator is not a statistical guarantee or cryptographic randomness. The first path is shown, not selected for its result.

Wealth calculations use log space. Terminal wealth depends only on win count; analytical probabilities sum a binomial distribution. A finish within 1e-12 natural-log units of the start counts as neither below nor above it. Median uses the first win count reaching cumulative probability 0.5. No costs, minimum stakes, rounding, changing probabilities, correlations, taxes, limits or liquidity. The stake cap 95% excludes all-in ruin and leverage; it does not make the model safe.
